# B1. Board, move generation, perft

The first increment: the stateful board and a move generator that is right, proven by perft.

## What it is

- `src/tables.rs`: attack tables built at compile time. Rays in eight directions, knight, king and pawn steps. Sliding attacks stop at the first blocker by bit scan; no magic bitboards yet.
- `src/board.rs`: `Board` is the actor. Bitboards by color and piece, side to move, castling rights, en passant square, the clocks, and an undo stack. `make(Move)` and `unmake()` are its messages; nothing is copied going up and down a tree. FEN in and out. `is_attacked(sq, by)` is the one attack question everything else asks.
- `src/movegen.rs`: pseudo-legal generation from the bitboards, then each move is made, the mover's king checked, and the move unmade. Pins, en passant discoveries and castling through check all fall out of that one check.
- `src/perft.rs` and `src/bin/perft.rs`: node counts and `--divide`.
- `tests/gates.rs`: the gates.

## Gates

Perft counts for the six standard positions, depths 1 to 4 or 5, all exact. `cargo test --release` runs them. `cargo test --release -- --ignored` adds one deeper count per position (119,060,324 for the start position at depth 6, and the rest).

A gate names the position and the depth when it fails, and checks that the board is unchanged afterwards.

## Predictions for the first build on the Mac

1. All six gates pass at every listed depth, and the deep set passes.
2. `perft 5` from the start position runs above 20 million nodes per second in release.
3. `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` is clean.

## Not in this increment

Evaluation, search, the graph, UCI. B2 brings the block model over from RUSE: `Block` with `control` per ply and `process` over a position, `BlockInfo` with typed arguments and meters, the expression language with chess built-ins in place of `rate` and `bpm`, and `chain`, `parallel`, `bypass`, `mix` and `only` over the two sides as channels.
