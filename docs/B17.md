# B17. Her side talks less: a cooldown, and no development or room lines

After playing her on B16, Brian wrote (25 October) that it "still feels to me like too many narrations and little depth". The numbers agree on the first half. In the B16 replay of 39 games her own lane (Me) speaks on 68% of her moves, 720 lines on 1,063 moves, against 0.21 lines per move pair for the Game lane and 0.16 for the You lane. Her lines by kind:

| Kind | Lines |
|---|---|
| I am attacking your X, which is loose | 198 |
| I developed my knight (or bishop) to X | 125 |
| I took your loose X | 101 |
| I moved my loose X out of danger | 92 |
| I played X to give my pieces more room (and the other block purposes) | 72 |
| Who is ahead, alone | 50 |
| A forced mate, for her or against her | 36 |
| I defended my X | 18 |
| Open file, castling, a center pawn, a promotion | 20 |
| A loose-piece clause or a check, alone | 8 |

Brian picked a cooldown on 25 October ("i like this hold on to it") and on 26 October chose that threats wait for it and that two of her moves pass in silence between ordinary lines. This increment does that and drops the development and room lines. It does not add depth; that is B18 (a line of play in the judge).

## What she says now (Me lane, quiet voice)

1. **Always said, on the move it happens:** a forced mate, for her or against her; a capture of a loose piece; a promotion. Material changes hands or the game is ending.
2. **No longer said:** "I developed my knight to f3." and "I played pawn to e3 to give my pieces more room." with the other block purposes (make my king safer, improve my pawn structure, cramp your pieces, expose your king, weaken your pawns).
3. **Everything else waits:** an attack on a loose piece, a loose piece moved to safety, a piece defended, castling, a center pawn, an open file, a new loose piece with nothing else to say, and a change in who is ahead. Such a line is said only when she has made at least three moves since her last line, so two of her moves pass in silence between them. A line that waits is not kept for later. The one exception is who is ahead: a change of the standing word that was not said is said with her next line, as B16 already does for a line that ran out of room.

The clauses ("Your knight on c3 is loose.", "It gives check.") stay with the line they belong to.

## What stays the same

The Game and You lanes, the search, the judge, sparring, the `every` voice, the stale-line rule, takebacks and the bridge. The spectator greeting in `bot/config.yml` still says "It narrates each move in plain English.", which stopped being true with B16. My replacement, Brian's to change: "Hello. {me} is an experimental chess engine from Symbia Labs (https://symbia-labs.com). It speaks when something happens and says why."

## Choices of mine, named so you can move them

* `COOLDOWN = 3` of her moves, counted from her last Me line of any kind, the always lines included.
* The always list is mates, captures of a loose piece and promotions. Threats are not on it (Brian, 26 October).
* The development and room sentences are removed from the Me lane. `best_gain` and `Gain` stay in `src/why.rs`, unused, so they can come back as ordinary lines.
* A line the outbox drops as stale still counts as spoken for the cooldown, as it already does for the standing.

## Estimate before the code

Re-filtering the B16 lines on the 39 games by their text, with these rules, gives her lane on about 32% of her moves (340 lines) and about 85 of the 198 threat lines still said. This is text, not the code: it cannot tell which standing changes come back after a wait. The bands below are set around it.

**The two fixes.** Both are in `next_level`, which decides which standing word holds, and both were there in B16. B16 said a standing on the move the word changed, so a word that lagged the score never reached the chat. B17 says a standing that waited on her next line, with whatever word is held then, and the lag showed.

1. The word was held by the size of the score, not its side, so "I am slightly better" could hold at -24. Now the score is measured along the side that was ahead. Logged before coding as `chess.b17.prediction.fix`.
2. A jump of more than one word was compared only with the far word's edge, so a score that fell just short of it kept the old word ("about equal" at +182). Now it lands on the highest word whose edge it has passed by the hold. Logged before coding as `chess.b17.prediction.fix2`.

**Two run problems, both mine.** The first set B run was void: while it was starting engines from `target/release/uci`, I copied the B16 binary over that path to check something, and its engines died (broken pipe, 3,771 of 5,045 plies replayed). Runs now use their own copy of the binary. The checker also crashed on a reply clause that names a king move ("I can answer with king to …"), the same blind spot B16's checker had once; `check_answer` now knows the king.

**One of your games.** The first ten plies of JSW4IZGV give 5 lines in B16 and 3 in B17. "I played pawn to e6 to give my pieces more room." and "I developed my knight to f6." are gone. The threat to your queen on d3, the blunder grade and "I took your loose queen on d3. I am winning because I am up a queen." stay.

**The mock game's IndexError.** `hook_smoke` logs an `IndexError` from lichess-bot's own simulator thread when its scripted Scholar's mate runs out of moves. The same traceback is in the B11 to B14 smoke logs, so it predates B17.
