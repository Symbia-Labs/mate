# B15. Sparring: playing at the opponent's level

Symbia wins nearly every game she plays: 36 of 39 between 20 and 23 October, and 15 of 15 against players under 1200. The people who play her asked for a sparring partner and a tutor. This increment makes her a sparring partner. In casual games against people she plays near the opponent's level and keeps the game level, or about a pawn in her favor.

## What changes

**A chooser after the search** (`src/spar.rs`). The search runs as before and finds the best move and its score S, in centipawns from her side.

- If she is not clearly ahead, or the search sees a forced mate, she plays the best move.
- Only when she is clearly ahead does the chooser look at other moves. It scores the best move and the five most promising alternatives with one shallower search each, and drops the alternatives that give back more than a limit that depends on the opponent's rating.
- Of the rest it takes the move that leaves her closest to a target a little above level, and never one that leaves her below level.
- The limit is per move, so a large lead is given back over several moves, and a hanging queen is never on the list.

**"Level" is measured in Stockfish's terms.** Her own evaluation reads about 130 centipawns more optimistic than Stockfish's near level (see What the games showed). The chooser therefore works in her own score less `EVAL_BIAS`, which is 130: she starts giving back above +230 of her own score (+100 by Stockfish's count), aims for +160 to +200 (+30 to +70) and never leaves herself below +130 (level). The first version had no offset and aimed at a number that was slightly behind.

**The limit, by opponent rating** (centipawns she may give back in one move):

| Opponent rating | Limit |
|---|---|
| unknown | 100 |
| under 1000 | 250 |
| 1000 to 1199 | 200 |
| 1200 to 1399 | 160 |
| 1400 to 1599 | 120 |
| 1600 to 1799 | 80 |
| 1800 to 1999 | 50 |
| 2000 and over | 0 (full strength) |

**Casual games against people only.** Rated games and games against bots are played as before, at full strength. A draw handed over in a rated game would move the opponent's rating, and a rated game is a fair game.

**The bridge** (`bot/homemade.py`) sets three UCI options on the first move of each game when sparring applies: `Sparring`, `Opponent` (the opponent's rating) and `SparSeed` (from the game id, so a game replays the same way). The introduction gets one more line saying what she does, and the log gets one line, "Sparring on: opponent N, seed S". If the engine refuses the options she plays her best. With `Sparring` off, which is the default, the engine answers exactly as it did in B14.

**Time.** She plays her best when her clock is under 20 seconds, so a flag is never the price of a gift. The chooser costs about 156,000 extra nodes on a move it changes (about 0.1 s at Mac speed, about 0.2 s at the hosted speed), and its nodes and time are counted in the `info` line.

## What stays the same

The search, the graph, the judge, the narration, the coach and the takeback path are not edited. The judge still runs at full strength, so "I would play X" is her best move, not the move she chose.

## Choices of mine, named so you can move them

- `EVAL_BIAS` 130; the band starts at +100 by Stockfish's count and the target is +30 to +70 by seed.
- The chooser is off for the first 10 half-moves and when she has less than 20 seconds on the clock.
- Five alternatives, screened at depth 2, then compared with the best move at one ply less than the search depth (at most 4) and 30,000 nodes each. Both sides of a comparison use the same depth.
- "Winning by one" is read as ahead by about a pawn, since a chess result has no margin.

## Predictions (written before any code)

1. **Off means off.** With `Sparring` false, the 602-position fingerprint at depth 5 is byte-identical to `matches/fp_b14_mac.tsv`, and the coach replay digests equal B14's.
2. **The chooser acts only where it should.** With `Sparring` on, a move differs from the full-strength move only when the full-strength score is above the band, the game is past half-move 10, the score is not a forced mate and the clock allows. At rating 2000 and over nothing changes.
3. **The give-back is bounded.** Over the 602 positions at rating 1300, every changed move gives back at most 160 centipawns at the comparison depth, and the chooser's own estimate of her score after it is not below level.
4. **Same on both machines.** The sparring-on fingerprint (rating 1300, seed 1, depth 5) has the same digest on the Mac build and the Linux build.
5. **It is cheap.** A changed move costs at most 160,000 extra nodes, and no ladder game ends on time or with an illegal move.
6. **She plays down.** Against Stockfish 19 at `UCI_Elo` 1320, 1500 and 1700 (20 openings twice each, her `Opponent` set to the level), her score is between 45% and 80% at each level. In B4 it was 92.5%, 87.5% and 61.0%.
7. **She keeps it close.** In those games her median score over her own moves from half-move 20 is between 0 and +150 centipawns at 1320 and 1500, and the same games with `Sparring` off have a higher median.
8. **She does not give the games away.** She loses no more than 20% of the games at each level.
9. **The bridge decides right.** A casual game against a person rated 1472 sets `Sparring` true with `Opponent` 1472; a rated game, a game against a bot and an unreadable rating do not; an offline run through lichess-bot's mock site configures the engine without a warning; every introduction line is 140 characters or fewer.
10. **How it plays for people** is open until she has played them. The measure: the 39 games so far gave people 3 wins (8%); over the next 20 casual games against people the score they take against her should be at least 25%.

Two predictions were rewritten before the run that scored them, and the ledger holds both versions: p6 to p8 named a time control (10 seconds plus 0.1) at which the clock rule switches the chooser off for the whole game, so those 120 games were sparring-off games (`matches/b15_void_10s`); the rerun uses 60 seconds plus 0.6 and the same bands (`chess.b15.prediction.ladder-rerun`). After the first sparring ladder a second version was written with the offset and its own bands, again before it played (`chess.b15.prediction.v2`).

## What the games showed

**Her evaluation runs about 130 centipawns high.** Over 5,300 of her moves from half-move 20, a position she scores 0 to 100 has a median Stockfish view of -95 for her, 100 to 200 gives 0, 200 to 300 gives +107, 300 to 500 gives +449 and 500 to 800 gives +656. The first version aimed at +30 to +70 of her own, which is about -100 to -150 by Stockfish, so it walked her into positions she believed were level and Stockfish did not. The offset fixes the aim and not its cause: the missing placement and castling terms. When the graph changes, measure the offset again.

**The second version is a level partner at 1320 and 1500 and a coin flip at 1700.** Against Stockfish 1700 she keeps the game level (Stockfish's view -35) and loses half of them. Stockfish at a given `UCI_Elo` is not a Lichess player of that rating; the limits in the table are for human ratings and have not met a human yet.

**She gives back less than the limit.** When she changes a move the median give-back is 79 centipawns against a limit of 160, because the alternatives she compares are the five best by a depth-2 screen, which lose little. Choosing alternatives nearer the amount she wants to give is the next change; it was held back so that the offset could be read alone.

**The 20-second rule shapes every test.** At 10+0.1 the clock never reaches 20 seconds, so the first ladder said nothing about sparring. Bullet games of 1+0 pass 20 seconds on her clock near half-move 50, so there the chooser stops late in the game.

## Not in this increment

The opening book, the middlegame and endgame blocks (castling among them: she castled in 6 of her 39 games, her opponents in 28), the tutor changes (a quiet default, the why, a hint ladder, takebacks, chat commands, a memory of each player), a `!full` command to switch sparring off during a game, adjusting the level from how the game is going, and choosing alternatives nearer the amount she wants to give.
