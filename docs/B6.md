# B6. Narration: the bot says why it moved

The engine already reads every position through named meters. This increment puts one plain sentence per move into the game chat, built from those readings and nothing else.

## What it is

- `src/narrate.rs`: one function, `say(board, report, graph)`, that returns a sentence of at most 140 characters (Lichess's chat limit) for the move the search chose. It reads the loose-piece lists that the `loose` block already computes, the captured piece, and the score. It does not search again and does not change the move.
- `src/bin/uci.rs`: a `Narrate` option, a check box, off by default. When it is on, the engine prints `info string say: <sentence>` just before `bestmove`. When it is off, the output is the same as in B3 to B5.
- `bot/homemade.py`: a small lichess-bot engine class, `Narrated`, that runs the same `uci` binary, then posts the sentence to the player chat after the move has been sent. lichess-bot starts each game in its own process, so the hook has to be an engine class it loads by name, not a patch applied at start-up.
- `bot/config.yml` and `tools/bot.sh`: the config selects the class; the script copies `bot/homemade.py` beside lichess-bot, which looks for it there.
- `tools/narrate_replay.py`: runs the sentences for one game's positions through the real UCI path, so a game can be read back move by move.

## What the sentence says

In this order, the first that applies:

1. A forced mate, for either side.
2. A capture: "Taking your loose queen on d6." if the piece was loose, otherwise "Taking the knight on d6."
3. A new threat: pieces of the opponent that are loose after the move and were not before, named by square.
4. A rescue: the engine's own loose piece moved or defended.
5. An old threat: "Your bishop on f5 is still loose."
6. Nothing loose on either side: "Quiet move."

Then the score from White's side, as Lichess's analysis board shows it, and the depth: "Eval +1.3, depth 6."

## Predictions

1. The words do not touch the move. On the 300 positions in `tests/fixtures/puzzles_sample.json`, searched to depth 4, `bestmove` is identical with `Narrate` on and off, 300 of 300.
2. The sentences are true to the board. On the same 300 positions, every sentence is at most 140 characters, and every "<piece> on <square>" names the piece that stands on that square, on the side the sentence says ("your" is the opponent's, "my" is the engine's).
3. Hanging pieces get named. On the same 300 positions, where the fixture marks one loose piece, at least 90% of the sentences name that square.
4. It would have said something about the game that started this. Searching each of Symbia's moves in game d1c4L3tW to depth 5, the sentence for the move that takes Brian's queen calls that queen loose, and at least one earlier sentence names one of Brian's loose pieces.
5. In the first live game after the restart, the bot posts one sentence for every move it makes, the log shows no chat error, and the median time from sending the move to the chat call returning is under 300 ms.
6. Brian reads one live game's sentences and judges at least half of them a correct reason for the move, and none of them wrong in a way that would mislead.

Predictions 1 to 4 are checked by tests and the replay tool. Predictions 5 and 6 need a live game and Brian.

## Not in this increment

Narrating the opponent's moves, a verbose mode for spectators, explaining the quiet moves from the king, mobility and pawn meters, and any language model. The sentences come from a fixed set of templates over the meters, so the same position always gets the same words.

## Results

Predictions 1 to 4 were checked before the bot was restarted. All four held. The words ran to depth 4 on the 300 positions in the fixture, through the real `uci` binary.

| # | Prediction | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Same move with `Narrate` on and off | Held. 300 of 300 identical, and nothing printed with it off. |
| 2 | Sentences true to the board | Held. All 300 at most 140 characters, every named piece on the square the sentence gave, and the binary's sentence equal to the library's. |
| 3 | Hanging piece named | Held, narrowly. 273 of 300, or 91.0%, against a bar of 90%. |
| 4 | The replay of d1c4L3tW | Held. The sentence for 16...gxf3 is "Taking your loose queen on f3." Two moves earlier, 14...g4 says "Attacking your queen on f3 and knight on h3, which are loose." |

The 27 misses on prediction 3 are not noise. Sixteen are forced mates, where the sentence says "I see a forced mate" and leaves the loose piece out on purpose. Nine are captures of a different piece, one is a bishop-and-queen rescue, and one is a check; the check was a real defect, because it read "A quiet move." for a move that gives check. The sentence now says "Giving check." and the gate numbers did not change. The margin on prediction 3 is thin because mates are common in a puzzle set of hanging pieces, not because the naming is unreliable.

Two things the checks turned up, both outside the engine:

- `lichess-bot` loads its own test engines whenever it looks up a homemade one, and they import `ExampleEngine` from `homemade.py`. A `homemade.py` without that name would have failed every live game at start-up. `tools/hook_smoke.py` runs the class through lichess-bot's own mock of lichess.org and found this; the file now keeps the name.
- The replay shows the score from White's side, as Lichess's analysis board does. For a bot playing Black that reads as "Eval -2.2" while the bot is winning. It is correct and it is easy to misread, and it is Brian's call whether the chat should say it the other way. (Decided in B11: the chat now says who is ahead and how far the search looked, in words.)

### Live game (YUAvFqlO, 20 October)

Brian restarted the bot and played it as White. The bot, Black, made 24 moves and posted 24 sentences. The log shows no chat error. The time from the move being sent to the chat call returning had a median of 108 ms and a maximum of 262 ms. Prediction 5 held.

Prediction 6 was a judgment by Brian. His reply was "way better," compared with no narration; he did not go sentence by sentence, so it is scored held on that word and nothing finer. His next request was for the same narration of his own moves, which is B7.
