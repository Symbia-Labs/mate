# B18. Two search defects: repetition parity and mate at the horizon

The MATE paper (Section 8, written in the mathematics project, 27 October) lists defects found by re-reading the code. Two are in the search that plays every move and that the judge uses for every grade. Both were confirmed in the source on 27 October (ledger `chess.finding.paper-defects`). Brian chose to fix these two first, with the line of play and the mate wording after (B19).

## The two defects

**Repetition is checked against half the game.** `Searcher::repeated` walks the game history with `.rev().skip(1).step_by(2)`. The last entry is the root, so the walk checks only the positions where the other side was to move. A position at an even ply of the search (her own side to move) is never compared with the game's earlier positions, only with the current line. She can walk into a repeated position she has already played, or away from one she should claim, without knowing. `Board::hash` includes the side to move, so comparing every earlier position is safe: a position with the wrong side to move cannot match.

**A mate on the last ply is missed.** `negamax` hands a node at depth 0 to `quiesce` before it looks for check. `quiesce` stands pat on the static score and searches captures only, so a side that is checkmated at the horizon gets the static score instead of the mate score. Measured on B17: in `6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/8/R5K1 w` the depth-1 search plays g1h2 at +2.08 and only depth 2 finds Ra8 mate; in `7k/8/8/8/8/8/R7/1R4K1 w` the mate in two first appears at depth 4. The same gap affects the judge, which grades with the same search.

## The fixes

1. `repeated` compares the node with every earlier position of the game (one change: the `.step_by(2)` goes).
2. `quiesce` does not stand pat in check. A side in check at a quiescence node searches every legal move; with none it is mated, scored as `negamax` scores it (`-MATE + ply`). The ply guard against runaway lines stays.

Nothing else changes: move generation, the evaluation graph, the move ordering, the coach's words and sparring's rules.

## What changes on purpose

The search's numbers. Every position whose tree reaches a check at the horizon now searches the evasions there, so node counts move nearly everywhere and some scores and moves move with them. The reference fingerprint (40ed48a1…9599 through B17) is expected to change; B18 sets a new one. The judge's grades move where its searches meet a check at the horizon. Strength should not drop; whether it rises measurably in 200 games is doubtful.

## Predictions (written before any code)

1. **Mate at the horizon.** At depth 1, `6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/8/R5K1 w` gives a1a8 with a mate-in-1 score (B17: g1h2, +208). At depth 3, `7k/8/8/8/8/8/R7/1R4K1 w` gives a mate-in-2 score (B17: depth 4).
2. **Repetition.** With game history [A, B, R], `repeated` finds both A and B (B17: only B), and a position not in the history is not found.
3. **Move generation untouched.** Every perft test passes.
4. **The fingerprint moves as expected.** Over the 602 reference positions at depth 5: the node count differs from B17 in at least half the rows; the best move differs in at most 10% of rows; the total node count rises by 0% to 20% from 106,989,330.
5. **The words stay true.** On the 39 games of set A (B16/B17's set), with B18's own search filling a new tutor cache: 0 wrong claims and 0 missing mates, loose captures or promotions in `tools/quiet_stats.py`; the Me lane still speaks on 25% to 38% of her moves.
6. **The grades move a little.** In the 602-position coach replay (`voice=every`, the judge at depth 6): between 0% and 15% of the 590 You lines differ from B17's, and 0 are wrong about the board.
7. **No loss of strength.** Against Stockfish 19 at UCI_Elo 1900, 10 s + 0.1 s, 200 games each (100 openings, both colors, the same openings for both builds): B18's score is within 10 points of B17's. The 95% interval of a difference between two such runs is about ±10 points, so this is a check against a regression, not a claim of a gain.
8. **Gates.** Library tests pass with the new ones (mate at depth 1, mate in 2 at depth 3, repetition both parities); `cargo fmt` and `clippy` clean; the browser build compiles.

Confidence that 1 to 8 all hold: about 50%. The likeliest breaks are 4 (the bands are guesses about how often checks reach the horizon) and 7 (noise in 200 games).

Confidence was about 50% that all eight would hold; three broke as written (4, 6 and 8), two of them on bands that were guesses.

**What it means for play.** She now sees a mate one ply sooner and every repeat of an earlier position. In 200 games against Stockfish at 1900 that did not move her result by a measurable amount, and it did not cost anything. It does cost about 30% more nodes to reach the same depth, which matters more on the hosted machine's two cores than on the Mac.

**What it means for the chat.** Grades change for about one move in five in the replay set, mostly between neighbouring classes; none is wrong about the board.
