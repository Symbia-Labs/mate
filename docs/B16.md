# B16. The tutor voice: quiet by default, and a reason for what she says

Brian played her on the B15 build (game Fr3sLJTY, 23 October) and wrote: "it still narrates every move and doesn't say why." He was right on both counts. Replaying that game through the coach gives 61 lines for 41 half-moves, three for every move pair. "I made a quiet move." opens 6 of her 21 lines and "I looked 3 moves ahead." ends 19 of them. A quiet move such as b3, Bb2 or Na3 never gets a reason. The reasons that do exist are tactical facts (a piece is loose, a capture, a check), and the "why" was deferred in B13 and again in B15.

This increment makes her quiet when nothing happened, and makes every line she does say carry a reason she can prove from the board.

## What a reason is

A reason is a fact that is true of the board and that the engine already computes, or can compute from its own blocks. She says nothing she cannot check. There are two kinds.

**A tactical fact.** A piece is loose, a capture takes a loose piece, a move attacks a loose piece, a piece is rescued, a king is checked, a mate is forced. These are the sentences B6, B7 and B13 already write.

**A change in one of the evaluation blocks.** Her evaluation is the sum of five blocks (material, loose, king, mobility, pawns). Run on its own, each block gives a number in centipawns for each side. A quiet move that raises the mover's mobility by 20 centipawns or more "gives my pieces more room"; one that lowers the mover's king exposure by 25 or more "makes my king safer"; one that raises the pawn term by 20 or more "improves my pawn structure". This is the reason her evaluation itself would give, which is why the graph is built from readable blocks.

Three plain opening facts also count, because they are textbook and can be checked from the move alone: a knight or bishop leaves its starting square in the first 20 moves ("I developed my knight"), a pawn reaches d4, e4, d5 or e5 ("I took space in the center"), and a rook arrives on a file with no pawns on it ("I put my rook on an open file").

## What she says now

**Me lane (her move).** In order, the first that applies is the main sentence:

1. A forced mate: "I see a forced mate in 3." (as before)
2. A capture of a loose piece: "I took your loose knight on c3." (as before) A capture of a protected piece is not said.
3. A promotion, a new attack on a loose piece, a loose piece moved to safety, a piece defended: as before.
4. Castling: "I castled kingside to keep my king safe."
5. Development, the center, an open file, or a block change, in that order, from the list above.
6. Nothing: the lane stays silent for this move.

A plain check is not said unless something else is. Clauses about loose pieces ("Your knight on c3 is loose.", "My bishop on c3 is loose.") are kept. The tail of the old line, who is ahead and how far she looked, now appears only on the move where the word for who is ahead changes ("equal", "slightly better", "better", "clearly better", "winning"), and it carries a reason: the material she is up ("I am up a knight.") or, when material is level, the block that leads.

**Game lane (your move).** It speaks only when your move left a piece of yours loose or attacked a loose piece of hers, and says what it did, as before. A capture, a check or a castle that did neither is on the board and is not repeated. "Still loose" is not repeated.

**You lane (the grade).** Best and Good moves are not graded aloud. An inaccuracy, a mistake, a blunder, a missed mate or an allowed mate is, as before, with a reason added when there is one: the capture she could make, what her best answer does when it is not a capture (it gives check, or it attacks a loose piece of yours that was not loose before: "I can answer with rook to a1, which gives check."; added after the first runs, see Results), or the block that your move made worse than the best move would have ("It leaves your king more exposed.", "It cramps your pieces.", "It weakens your pawns.").

## What stays the same

The search, the graph, the judge's verdicts and costs, the sparring chooser, the takeback path and the stale-line rule are not touched. `uci coach voice=every` gives back the B15 lines exactly; the bot asks for `quiet` unless the environment sets `SYMBIA_VOICE=every`. A silent lane writes `skip <ply> <lane>` so a reader that counts lines can tell silence from a delay. The Python bridge logs it and posts nothing. The first introduction line changes to say how she talks now.

## Choices of mine, named so you can move them

* The thresholds: 20 (mobility), 25 (king), 20 (pawns) centipawns, mover's view, comparing the position after her move with the position before it (the judge: after your move with after the best move).
* Development counts in the first 20 moves of the game. The opening facts are three and are exact. Everything else is a block change.
* A change in who is ahead is a change in the word, not in the number.
* Plain captures of protected pieces are silent, so a trade is not announced. The cost is that a plain win of a pawn goes unsaid until it moves the standing word.
* `voice=every` is kept as a switch for tests and for anyone who wants every line. A chat command to change the voice during a game is not in this increment.

## What the games showed

**Fr3sLJTY, the game you played.** The same 41 plies now come out as 31 lines instead of 61. Her side reads "I played pawn to e3 to give my pieces more room.", "I developed my bishop to b2.", "I am better because I am up a pawn.", "I moved my loose bishop out of danger.", "I played rook to d1 to use the open d-file. It gives check." Your side is silent on your good moves and speaks eight times, three of them with the new clause.

**A check is a thing she can do, not proof of why the move cost what it did.** In that game "I can answer with castling queenside, which gives check." appears at plies 28, 32 and 36: true each time, and the same each time, because the position that allows it did not change. It reads as a reason and is weaker than one. A real cause needs a line of play, and the judge keeps only the best answer, not the line.

**Her side is still the chattiest.** She speaks on 68% of her moves (720 of 1,063), mostly "I developed my knight to f3." and "I played X to give my pieces more room." Each is true and each is the kind of line you may still find constant; p11 is your reading.

**Most of the cost of a bad move is not in any block.** 95 of the 166 inaccuracies and worse still name no cause. The blocks are mobility, king, pawns, material and loose pieces; a move that gives up a good square, a diagonal or the bishop pair costs real points and none of them see it.

**She cannot say that castling made her safer.** The king block charges 30 centipawns for a king shut in on its back rank while the other side has a rook or queen, so castling raises her own exposure term and the honest sentence is "I castled kingside." This is the root of the castling finding (`chess.finding.castling`), not a new one.
