# B11. Saying the score and the depth in words

The bot ends most of its sentences with a number, such as "Eval -2.9, depth 7." Two things make that hard to read in a game chat. The number is in pawns from White's side, so a bot playing Black that is winning reports a negative score (B6 recorded this and left the decision to Brian). And "depth 7" means seven half-moves of search, which is not how anyone counts moves at the board. This increment replaces both with plain words. It changes no chess: the search, the score and the move are untouched, and only the sentence about them changes.

## What changes

The tail of a sentence about the bot's own move had the form `Eval +0.6, depth 5.` It now has two short sentences.

**Who is ahead.** The score is read from the side that just moved, the bot, and said in five steps:

| Score for the bot (centipawns) | Sentence |
|---|---|
| under 30 either way | The position is about equal. |
| 30 to 79 | I am slightly better. / You are slightly better. |
| 80 to 179 | I am better. / You are better. |
| 180 to 349 | I am clearly better. / You are clearly better. |
| 350 or more | I am winning. / You are winning. |

The cut points follow the usual annotation symbols: a few hundredths of a pawn is noise, about a third of a pawn is a slight edge, a pawn or more is a clear one, and three and a half pawns decides a game between players of this strength. They are a choice and not a measurement, and they are named constants so that Brian can move them.

**How far it looked.** The engine reports depth in plies. The sentence says `I looked N moves ahead.`, where N is the depth divided by two and rounded up, so an odd depth, which ends on the bot's own move, counts that move. Depth 5 is "3 moves ahead", depth 6 is also "3", and depth 1 or 2 is "1 move ahead". The sentence says what the search did and no more: it does not count the captures the search follows past its nominal depth.

The two sentences come in the same order of preference as before, and each is dropped whole when the line would pass Lichess's 140 characters: both, then the first alone, then neither. A forced mate is still said as a mate and carries neither. The second introduction line, which explained "Eval numbers are in pawns from White's side", is replaced by one that says the bot gives who is ahead and how far it looked.

## What stays the same

The score is still the engine's own number, so a line that says "I am winning" is the evaluation graph speaking. It is not a second opinion. The sentence about the opponent's move (`say_theirs`) has no score and is not touched.

## Predictions

Logged on the ledger before any run.

1. **The chess does not change.** Over the 602 positions of the sample games at depth 5, the move, depth, score, node count and principal variation are identical to the B8 reference run; only the sentence column differs.
2. **No numbers in the tail.** No sentence contains "Eval", "depth", "pawns" or a signed decimal such as "+0.6", and the introduction no longer mentions White's side.
3. **The side is right.** In every position, the sentence names the side the score favors, worked out from the score column and not from the code: "I am" for a positive score, "You are" for a negative one, "about equal" inside 30 centipawns, and the size word matches the band.
4. **The depth is said honestly.** For each depth from 1 to 12, the words are `ceil(depth / 2)` moves, and the singular is used for 1.
5. **The words fit.** No sentence passes 140 characters. The "who is ahead" sentence survives in at least 99 percent of the 579 sentences that are not mates, and both sentences survive in at least 90 percent. (Before, the figure was 579 of 579 with the number and depth.) About 70 percent sure; the new tail is about 45 characters against 19.
6. **The claims still hold.** `tools/narrate_check.py` finds no false claim in the sample games and reports the same recall as in B6 and B7, and the Rust gates pass.
7. **It still loads.** The offline smoke test through lichess-bot's own mock site passes with the new introduction, and every introduction line is at most 140 characters.
8. **The Linux build agrees.** The new musl build's fingerprint equals the Mac build's, over all 602 positions, sentences included (the B8 result, repeated because the sentence column changed).
9. **It reads well in a game.** After the update reaches the hosted bot, Brian plays a game and does not ask for the wording to change. This needs a push, which needs his yes for the firewall step, and a game.

The update went to the hosted bot as release `81c66467f209` on the first push attempt: the bot was idle, the install took 16 seconds, port 22 was open for 31 seconds, and Lichess shows Symbia online on the same instance. It needed no token and no rebuild.

The reference fingerprint for the sentence-bearing run is now `783e378f…974f`. The B8 and B9 value, `3227240a…d9f9`, belongs to the wording before this increment, and the two differ only in the sentence column.

Tests: five existing tests in `src/narrate.rs` were rewritten for the new wording, and three were added (the cut points, the depth table, and a check that no digit-and-sign score is left). The score is now read from the side that moved, so one test that checked a Black score from White's side became a pair that checks it from the mover's side in both directions.

Of the 579 sentences in the sample, the bot says "I am winning" in 129 and "You are winning" in 110, which reflects the sample and not the engine's strength: the positions are from Brian's own games, and the engine is asked for a move in every position of each, including positions where one side is far ahead.

## Not in this increment

The numbers are gone from the chat, so nobody can read the exact score there. A spectator-room line with the number, or a command that asks for it, would restore it for anyone who wants it. Sentences about the position in terms other than material and loose pieces (space, king safety, pawn structure) wait for blocks that compute them.
