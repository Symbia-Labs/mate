# B13. The You lane: Symbia's engine judges the student's move

Brian chose Symbia's own engine as the judge of his moves, and said the judging must run as its own loop, apart from the game narration and from Symbia's own move. B12 built the lane and left it silent. This increment gives it a search and a verdict.

## What the lane has to do

After Symbia moves, the student is thinking. That time is free. After the student moves, Symbia starts its own search at once, and nothing the You lane does may slow it. So the lane has two jobs on two different clocks.

1. **Ponder** (on the `tutor` event). The position after Symbia's move is the one the student must answer. Search it while he thinks, and keep the engine's best move and score for each depth that finished.
2. **Judge** (on the `student` event). Compare the student's move with the best move. If it is the best move, say so; there is nothing to search. Otherwise search the position after his move one ply shallower and compare the two scores. The difference, in centipawns, is what the move cost.

## Design

**One pure function.** The verdict for a move is a function of the position, the move, a depth and a node limit, and of nothing else. The ponder is only a cache of the first half of it. A ponder that was cut short is thrown away, not used, so the words never depend on how long the student thought or how busy the machine was. Both searches are limited by depth and nodes, never by the clock.

**The ponder yields.** The hook sends the `student` event before Symbia's search starts. The coach raises a flag on that event (and on a takeback), and the ponder's search stops at its next check, about every 1,000 nodes. The judge uses its own searcher, which the flag does not reach, so a second `student` event arriving while a verdict is being built cannot spoil it.

**Reading the cost.** Scores are clamped to plus or minus 1,000 centipawns before they are subtracted, so winning a little more or a little less in a won position costs nothing.

| Cost | Class |
|---|---|
| the best move, or within 10 | Best |
| under 50 | Good |
| 50 to 119 | Inaccuracy |
| 120 to 299 | Mistake |
| 300 or more | Blunder |

**What the line says.** Symbia speaks as "I" and the student is "you", as in the Game lane. Best and Good get one short sentence. Inaccuracy, Mistake and Blunder say what the move cost in pawns, what Symbia would do in reply if that reply takes a piece, and the move Symbia preferred. A move that allows a mate names the mate; a move that misses one says it had one. Sentences come from fixed templates, longest first, and the longest one that fits 136 characters is used, as in `narrate`.

**What the lane cannot know.** The coach sees one position, not the game, so the judge does not know about repetition or the fifty-move count. It searches with the default graph. Both are limits of this increment, not of the design.

**Settings.** `uci coach` takes `depth=` and `nodes=` for the measurements; the defaults are depth 6 and 500,000 nodes. The hook is unchanged. The introduction in the bot is rewritten to explain the three tags.

## What stays the same

The search, the evaluation, the engine's moves and node counts (the search module is not edited). The Game and Me lines. The outbox rules: a You line more than one ply behind is dropped, and a takeback voids it.

## Predictions

Logged on the ledger before any code or run.

1. **Labeled cases come out as labeled.** Ten moves, labeled before the code exists. Five are clear: taking a free queen is Best; a quiet move that misses a mate in one is a Blunder that says it had a mate; 3...Nf6 in the Scholar's mate line is a Blunder that names the mate; a queen moved onto an attacked square with no defender is a Blunder that says I can take it; and the engine's own best move is Best with a cost of 0. I expect all five. Five are quiet moves from the start position (e4, d4, a3, f3, g4); I expect e4 and d4 to be Best or Good, a3 to be anything but Mistake or Blunder, f3 to be worse than e4, and g4 to be worse than a3. I expect at least 4 of the 5 quiet ones, and 80 percent sure of all 5.
2. **The words do not depend on the clock.** Over the 602 reference positions, the coach writes byte-identical You lines whether each `student` event comes at once after the `tutor` event (the ponder is always cut off) or after a pause long enough for the ponder to finish. About 85 percent sure; the risk is a node limit that is reached at the same moment as the flag.
3. **The ponder yields.** With a budget of 50 million nodes, the ponder returns within 25 ms of the flag in 20 of 20 tries, and returns a cut-off result that the judge discards.
4. **Verdicts arrive in time.** On the Mac, with the ponder finished, the You line is out within 0.5 s of the `student` event for 95 percent of the 602 positions and within 2 s for all of them. Cold, with no ponder, within 1.5 s for 95 percent.
5. **The verdict is not noise.** Judging the same 602 played moves at depth 5 and at depth 6, the class is the same for at least 80 percent. This is a measurement, not a pass mark for the product; if it fails, the thresholds are too fine for the depth and the gap goes on the list.
6. **Every line is whole and true.** Every You line is tagged and at most 140 characters, and every piece it names stands on the square it names in the position it is about.
7. **Symbia's own search does not slow.** With the coach busy judging beside it, the engine's nodes per second in a fixed 1 s search stay at 95 percent or more of the engine alone, measured with the two on separate cores. The hosted machine has 2 vCPUs, so this is its real case. Pinned to one shared core the engine will lose speed; I will measure it and say by how much.
8. **Nothing else moved.** The reference fingerprint is still `783e378f…974f` over 106,989,330 nodes, and an idle coach uses under 0.05 s of CPU in 30 s.
9. **The Linux build agrees.** The digest over every line the coach writes for the 602 positions, You lines included, is the same on the Mac and on the musl build.
10. **It reads right in a game.** After a push and a game, Brian reads Game, You and Me lines that are in order, that he agrees with, and that do not feel like noise. This needs his yes for the firewall step and a game.

## Not in this increment

- A top-moves list (MultiPV). The judge needs only the best move and one more search, so a list would cost time and add nothing to the verdict.
- Saying why a move is bad beyond the reply and the better move. The blocks can name loose pieces already; joining them to the verdict is for later.
- Rating-aware thresholds. One table serves every student.
- Game history in the judge (repetition, fifty-move count).
